The Agent Your Company Hires Will Be Your Own

A couple of days ago I came across a really cool project called HashAgent. You open a web page and there’s an agent inside it, running in your browser, calling your local GPU through WebGPU — no server, no setup needed. The most special part is that this agent can be saved as a URL. You tweak its prompts, its tools, its boundaries, then send out the link; whoever opens it runs the exact version you tuned. Agents are becoming a lightweight unit you can carry, share, and deploy with zero friction. And once an agent becomes that kind of unit, it is naturally “personal.” I’ve long believed the future of hiring will shift from hiring individuals to hiring individuals’ agents — this project showed me that my prediction is actually achievable.

What’s inside an agent unit

An agent that can be saved as a URL and run locally carries more than just capability — it carries the traces of the person who wrote it.

Which tools you picked, which data sources you connected. What boundaries you set for the model, what it’s not allowed to do on its own. What kind of answers you prefer, and how you told it to handle uncertainty. Put together, that’s the agent’s “personality.” Two people using the same model will tune out different agents, because the base is the same but the person written into it is different.

This confirms a point I’ve wanted to make for a while: a technical person’s edge is shifting from “what tools you can use” to “whether you can write your taste and way of working into something reusable.”

The future form of agents is a unit that carries your personal imprint and can be packaged and taken with you — not yet another bigger model.

The unit of hiring has changed

So how does “hiring” change? Let me put the two units side by side.

Dimension Hire a person Hire a person’s agent
How you carry it Resume, interview, probation A URL, a config, a runnable piece of code
Can you verify in advance Hard — you only know after working together Yes — throw it a task and watch it run
Who sets the boundaries Contract and conscience Written into the agent’s prompts and permissions
What leaves when they quit The person and the experience leave together The config stays; the next place runs the same unit
Cost to replace Recruit, train, ramp up Swap the base model; behavior stays the same

This table isn’t meant to prove people will be replaced. It’s saying that once an agent becomes a portable, verifiable, boundary-locked unit, what a company buys is no longer “a person’s time.” What it buys is a proven capacity to execute. And that capacity wears the shape of the person who wrote it.

What a company hires will become “the person plus their unique agent.” The person owns judgment and accountability; the agent turns judgment into stable, reproducible action. The combination of the two is the new unit of work.

Why it has to live on your side

This also explains why I weigh “local agents” so heavily. For an agent to run carrying your imprint, it can’t live on a server you don’t control. WebGPU running in the browser, the model residing locally — these look like tech preferences, but underneath is a harder requirement. Your agent holds your taste and your boundaries; it has to run where you control it.

The rise of local models and “the agent as a personal unit” are two ends of the same force. One end: the model can run on your own machine. The other end: the agent can be packaged and taken with you.

Some will say this is just “AI replaces people” in different words. My take: at most it replaces “the person who only sells their time.” Someone who only repeats execution and leaves no imprint of their own will indeed be replaced by a written agent. But someone who writes their taste, judgment, and workflow into an agent becomes more valuable than before, because now they can run ten such units at once and produce ten times what they used to.

There’s one spot I haven’t figured out a mature answer for yet: as a “personal unit,” an agent can be carried — which also means it can be stolen or copied. Your taste becomes a file; someone else copies it and uses it. I have no answer for this today, because even short-video plagiarism still can’t be properly protected, and agents will likely be harder.
